
### Question 1: **Why do you want to study this course or subject?**

    First and foremost, I am fascinated by the process in which vehicles concerned with aerodynamics are designed, built and tested in the real world. This is not only driven by my passion for aviation and the design of aircraft, but also my enjoyment of problem solving and creating and managing complex systems.

    After University, I aspire to enter a career in the aerospace industry, where I am keen to develop our understanding and mathematical models of aerodynamics to maximise aerodynamic efficiencies, requiring intricate knowledge in and experience with varying fields of aerospace engineering, as well as a strong knowledge of the general engineering project lifecycle, and I believe that studying an Aerospace Engineering course will aptly prepare me as such.

### Question 2: **How have your qualifications and studies helped you to prepare for this course or subject?**

    I am currently studying four A-Levels: Physics, Mathematics, Further Mathematics and Computer Science.
    My studies in Physics have provided me with a knowledge of mechanics that will be foundational to my further studies in Aerospace Engineering.

    Mathematics and Further Mathematics have given me thorough experience in mathematical modelling for real-world scenarios, and some more advanced mathematical concepts that will be foundational to understanding the mathematics involved in the various aerodynamic forces.

    The Computer Science A-Level has given me thorough experience in programming and software architecture, crucial for both developing aerospace-related software and using computational analysis tools such as MATLAB. Computer Science has also given me a foundational understanding of the low-level operations of computer systems, which is useful for optimising program code in unmanaged languages commonly used in aerospace applications, for example in guidance/telemetry microcontroller programming.

### Question 3: **What else have you done to prepare outside of education, and why are these experiences useful?**

    I participated in the 2026 UK CanSat competition, in which I worked with four other A-Level students over five months to design, build and test a miniaturised satellite simulation called a “CanSat” alongside my A-Level studies.
    As part of the team, I designed and wrote the code used in the CanSat’s microcontroller, contributed to the team’s Preliminary and Critical Design Review with high-level documentation of the code, participated in field testing of the CanSat's radio telemetry system and performed time-pressured troubleshooting of the CanSat’s systems on launch day.

    The CanSat competition gave me fascinating insight into and experience with the lifecycle of an aerospace-related engineering project.
    
    With this new insight into aerospace, my interest in learning more about the industry was piqued.
    As such, I was thrilled to become aware of a work experience placement opportunity at GE Aerospace.
    During the placement, I worked with a team of 4 A-Level students in a data science project involving selecting and fine-tuning a machine learning model to predict when components of a turbofan engine would need to be replaced.
    The data science work also included finding and removing outliers from the various categories of engine sensor data based on an understanding of realistic operating conditions for the engine.
    Along with the group project, we were also provided with a tour of the aerospace equipment manufacturing facilities on-site, where I was able to ask questions to industry professionals about the hardware being produced on-site, as well as their pathway taken to their career.
    My team and I presented a documentary of our process in producing our machine learning model to industry professionals and other teams at the end of the placement.
